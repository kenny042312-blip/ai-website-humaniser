# AI Website Humaniser 1.0

Make the website feel like its design follows from its audience, content and task. Treat “human-made” as a subjective design judgment, not a visual formula or a claim about who made the page.

Read [the research basis and limits](references/research-basis.md) when using findings from the website-directory study.

## Approach

1. **Find the page's real job.** Use the user's brief to identify the audience, primary task, context, and information the visitor needs to decide or act. If a detail is missing, make a reversible assumption when possible; ask only when the choice would substantially change the site.
2. **Shape the content before choosing modules.** Organize the actual material for the task: a timetable, comparison, article, directory, product explanation, dashboard, or other fit-for-purpose structure. Do not default to a hero followed by equal cards.
3. **Give the page a coherent visual point of view.** Choose type, color, spacing, image treatment, density and composition that suit the subject. Vary rhythm where the content changes. Use cards, gradients, symmetry, serif type, asymmetry, texture, or motion when they serve the work; none proves or disproves human authorship. Do not add arbitrary quirks or distressed decoration as a shortcut to authenticity.
4. **Use specific, supportable content.** Prefer concrete names, units, examples, constraints, dates and actions when the source provides them. Do not invent testimonials, statistics, prices, availability, credentials or business history. Clearly label invented content as a sample or fictional detail.
5. **Make controls earn their place.** A visible control should produce a clear, observable result. Make filters filter, tabs switch real content, search find relevant items, and sample actions explain their effect. Keep labels, focus states, keyboard use, contrast, reduced-motion behavior and narrow layouts in the design.
6. **Compare decisions cleanly when experimentation helps.** Hold the brief and content constant; change one visual dimension at a time, such as table versus cards or a different type pairing. Label the variants and summarize the difference. Let the user decide which direction fits.
7. **Review the result in context.** Inspect the actual page at wide and narrow sizes. Check the primary task, key interactions and visible layout problems. Fix issues that interfere with clarity or use; do not claim that a visual review establishes authorship.

## Handoff

Present the page or preview first. Briefly name the visual choices and working interactions, note which content is fictional or still needs real source data, and invite feedback about specific elements. Follow the available website-building and publishing workflow when the user requests a hosted site; a style preference or sample build does not authorize a public release by itself.

# Landing pages: make a coherent, supported argument

Use for a landing page, campaign, offer or relevant copy revision. A homepage may serve several intents; a focused landing page usually serves one dominant visitor decision. Do not impose this structure on an operational screen or narrow style edit.

Adapted from A1–A8 of [Elaya's landing-page skill](https://github.com/elayadesign/ai-design-skills/blob/4fdcdd52f15eda09da1257c303c860c13e0c879f/skills/landing-page-design/SKILL.md#part-a--strategy-and-structure). These are working hypotheses for this brief, not measured conversion laws. See [attribution](../THIRD_PARTY_NOTICES.md).

## Resolve the decision before the sections

Use supplied material, the existing site and connected sources first. Identify these facts in compact working notes; ask only for consequential unknowns, grouped together. Reversible layout assumptions can be stated and used. Unknown commercial claims cannot be filled in as assumptions.

| Question | Design consequence |
|---|---|
| What does the visitor receive, and who is it for? | The lead identifies the actual offer and audience |
| What counts as success: download, booking, signup, purchase or something else? | The primary action leads to that outcome; a click is not automatically a completed conversion |
| Where does the visitor arrive from, and what promise do they see there? | Preserve an accurate campaign promise and visual continuity; do not claim a traffic source without evidence |
| What does the visitor already understand? | Familiar offers can be short; unfamiliar ones need explanation before commitment |
| What prevents a decision? | Put cost, effort, compatibility, availability or another documented objection near the decision it affects |
| What proof and assets exist? | Prefer a relevant example, demonstration or authorized quote beside the claim it supports |
| Which brand, platform and mobile constraints are fixed? | Preserve them rather than silently replacing the user's accepted direction |

Use a small claim ledger when a page contains material promises:

| Claim or decision | Source/status | Allowed treatment |
|---|---|---|
| A product function confirmed by the supplied specification | Verified for that version | Demonstrate it with accurate labels |
| Price, cancellation, guarantee or quantified result without a source | Unknown | Omit the claim or keep an explicit internal placeholder; resolve before public launch |
| A fictional customer's record in a demo | Invented sample | Label the demo clearly and keep its internal data consistent |

Do not invent testimonials, names presented as real endorsements, logos, customer counts, risk reversals or urgency. If proof is missing, an honest demonstration can be the lead evidence. Do not manufacture social proof to complete a template.

## Select a page argument

| Structure | Fits when | What to emphasize | What to avoid |
|---|---|---|---|
| Product explanation | A representative screenshot or workflow explains the offer | Clear lead, product evidence, benefits, necessary details and next step | Decorative mockups with no connection to the claim |
| Explanatory story | The visitor needs context or has substantial objections | Problem, mechanism, evidence, limits and decision in a useful order | A long scroll that withholds essential facts |
| Minimal action page | A familiar audience arrives for a specific download, invitation or signup | The offer, prerequisites and a short path to the outcome | Adding a full sales funnel to a simple action |
| Comparison | The visitor needs to distinguish alternatives | Shared attributes, units, inclusions, exclusions and dated sources | Invented competitor shortcomings or unequal comparisons |

This choice is not a four-template restriction. Combine forms when the content requires it. Record why the selected structure fits the visitor's knowledge and decision.

For a substantial new offer page, an outline can contain: opening promise and action; product or process evidence; meaningful benefits; how it works; proof; unresolved objections; relevant terms; another opportunity to take the same action. Include only warranted sections, in the order that answers the visitor's questions. The source's three steps, three-to-five benefits and six-to-twelve FAQs are drafting prompts, not content quotas. Do not invent steps or pad questions to reach them.

Above the first scroll, aim to establish what the page offers and the next useful step. Show proof or a visual there when it helps and actually exists. Do not force every device to fit a heading, two paragraphs, image, proof strip and button into one screen by shrinking essential text. A repeated CTA should retain the same destination and promise. A subordinate detail or sign-in link can remain when it serves a different legitimate need.

## Write usable copy

Describe the outcome and its mechanism together. “Review the changed stock counts before saving” explains an actual workflow; “transform your operations” does not. A measurable claim is stronger only when its unit, context and source are known. Never convert vague copy into an unsupported time saving or percentage.

Possible headline shapes include an outcome with a relevant constraint, a category for a defined audience, or a specific task and deliverable. Treat them as drafts to test with the real content. Do not force every brand into the same formula or include an unverified time promise.

- A subheadline clarifies what it is, who it serves and how it works; it need not restate the heading.
- An action label names its result: “Download the sample timetable,” “View available sessions,” or “Request a quote” when those outcomes are implemented. “Start free trial” requires a real free trial.
- A benefit pairs an outcome with a concrete capability or supported example. Avoid a feature list whose consequences remain unexplained.
- Put decisive terms at the action, not only in a footer. State existing trial, refund, cancellation or payment terms accurately; creating a guarantee is not a design decision.
- Use questions that visitors actually need answered. Move high-friction answers earlier instead of hiding them in a long FAQ.
- Use the project's voice. Sentence case and direct language are useful starting points, not permission to alter brand names, identifiers or source quotations. Preserve correct hyphens and other punctuation.
- State system outcomes calmly and specifically. An error identifies the problem and a recoverable next step. Do not report success before the actual operation succeeds.

Use complete draft sentences and representative content lengths. Fictional data needs coherent relationships: capacities constrain bookings, repeated people keep consistent identities, totals match rows, and dates match the scenario. Arbitrary decimals and irregular dates do not establish authenticity. A real round number remains a round number.

## Build and review in manageable passes

For a new landing page, draft the outline, lead copy, CTA, proof status and relevant objections before refining the visual system. Keep these as working notes unless the user requests a strategy deliverable. A plan-approval request still takes precedence.

Implement one representative section and its real interaction state, establish reusable roles, then extend the page. Revisit a shared token when several sections expose the same issue. Avoid rebuilding the whole page for each copy revision. Check that the product visual, surrounding claim and CTA describe the same outcome.

Use [shipping review](shipping-review.md) for search intent, metadata, routes and forms when publication is in scope. Read [visual review](visual-review.md) for rendered checks. Claim conversion improvement only from a suitable measurement, not from completing this process.
